# Module 8: Professional Financial Analysis Reporting

> [!abstract] Module Purpose
> Modules 2–7 taught you to read statements, compute ratios, judge earnings quality, map risk, forecast the future, and price it. Module 8 adds no new instruments — it adds the **deliverable**: a professional report that a committee, a lender, or an investor can act on, and the discipline to make every claim in it traceable. Two objectives, one arc: assemble the whole analysis into a structured argument (LO 8.1), then adapt that same evidence to the reader whose decision you are trying to move (LO 8.2).
>
> The running companies are **Sage Inc.** — the retailer you met in [[M6-Obj-6-1]] §V, now a company you must *write about* — and **Tesla, Inc.** (FY2025 Form 10-K), the course capstone: the same filing, read by four audiences, and written up twice.

## Learning Objectives

| Objective | Full Title | Analytical Focus |
|---|---|---|
| [[M8-Obj-8-1]] | Synthesize the 7-Step Financial Analysis Reporting Framework | The detective-story arc (causes → outcomes) · the seven steps and each one's *proof obligation* · industry → strategy → accounting quality → profitability &amp; risk → pro forma forecast → valuation → recommendation · executive-summary anatomy · footnote &amp; MD&amp;A cross-examination (the filing wins) · the Sage Inc. initiation exercise · the pre-submission checklist |
| [[M8-Obj-8-2]] | Tailor Financial Reports to the Reader and Deliver the Capstone | The four institutional audiences and their objectives, lead metrics and blind spots · what changes when you tailor (question, order, risk emphasis, decision requested) and what never changes (the evidence) · the Tesla capstone anchor across Modules 1–7 · the two-pack exercise: a lender-grade credit memo and an investor-grade equity thesis on the same filings · reconciliation of apparently conflicting conclusions · capstone deliverable, evidence standards, and grading rubric |

> [!success] Key Findings from the Working Examples
> - **A report is an argument, not a folder of answers.** The seven steps are only useful if each one *discharges a proof obligation* — the reader must be able to conclude what the step exists to establish. A report can cover all seven topics and decide nothing.
> - **The executive summary is written last and placed first**, because it must summarise claims the body actually supports, and because most readers read only it. It must stand alone.
> - **Management's narrative is checked against the filing, and the filing wins.** Where the MD&A and the footnotes disagree, the disagreement is itself a finding for Step 3.
> - **The same Tesla facts support four defensible documents.** Net cash of **\$35,683M**, 12.9× coverage, and 0.57-year payback make the credit trivial; **416.4×** reported and **661.1×** core earnings make the equity a bet on a future; **\$8,527M** of capex against **\$6,220M** of free cash flow and a **535M-share** issuance make it a capital-allocation question; and a peer 8.0× multiple implies an enterprise value **19.7× below** what the market pays.
> - **Tailoring is not spin.** The evidence must stay invariant across audiences; only the question, the framing, and the requested decision change. If the numbers change when the audience changes, the analyst is writing two stories.
> - **A lender's recommendation and an equity analyst's rating can both be right.** The facility is repaid from cash; the equity payoff is entirely the residual under a 661× multiple. The documents conflict only if one imports the other's conclusion.

## The Module 8 Analytical Framework

| Step | Technique | Question Answered | Where Applied |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Industry economic characteristics (structure, value chain, cyclicality) | Where does the firm compete, and what sets the industry's profit ceiling? | [[M8-Obj-8-1]] §II |
| 2 | Corporate strategic positioning and peer benchmarking | How does the firm compete, and is the position durable? | [[M8-Obj-8-1]] §II |
| 3 | Statement examination and earnings-quality assessment | Which reported figures can be relied upon? | [[M8-Obj-8-1]] §II |
| 4 | Profitability and risk diagnosis (common-size, Du Pont, coverage, CCC) | What do the numbers say about returns and fragility? | [[M8-Obj-8-1]] §II |
| 5 | Pro forma forecasting and the financing gap | Where is the plan heading, and what does it require? | [[M8-Obj-8-1]] §II |
| 6 | Valuation (FCFF/WACC, terminal value, multiples) | What are the projected cash flows worth, as a range? | [[M8-Obj-8-1]] §II |
| 7 | Closing remarks and recommendation | So what — and what would change it? | [[M8-Obj-8-1]] §II |
| 8 | Stakeholder mapping (equity · credit · board · M&A) | Whose decision are we trying to move? | [[M8-Obj-8-2]] §I |
| 9 | Audience adaptation with an invariant evidence base | What changes, and what must never change? | [[M8-Obj-8-2]] §I |
| 10 | Two-pack production (credit memo + equity thesis) | How do the same numbers serve two different readers? | [[M8-Obj-8-2]] §III |
| 11 | Capstone assembly, evidence standards, and audit | Can a reviewer reconstruct every number? | [[M8-Obj-8-2]] §IV |

> [!tip] From a Course of Instruments to a Profession
> Every earlier module ended with numbers: a ratio, a score, a forecast, a value. Module 8 ends with a **document** — and documents are judged by a different standard, because they are read by someone who did not do the work and will not redo it. The habits that carry over are the habits the course drilled: normalize before you multiply, disclose the assumption behind every projection, quote a range rather than a point, and let the filing overrule the narrative. The new habit is the one this module adds: **know your reader, because the same evidence asks for a different sentence first.**
