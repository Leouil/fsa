# Course Overview

This course is designed to teach you financial statement analysis the way it is actually practiced in the real world. Rather than learning isolated techniques, you will follow the workflow of a professional financial analyst — moving from understanding financial data to producing a clear, decision-useful analysis.

## The Learning Flow

The course follows a clear progression: **Skepticism → Measurement → Context → Interpretation → Prediction → Communication**

Each module builds on the previous one, so that by the end of the course, you can complete a full financial analysis from start to finish.

## Why This Course Is Organized This Way

In practice, financial analysts do not simply calculate ratios or memorize formulas. They:

- Question the reliability of financial information
- Analyze and interpret data within a business context
- Assess risks and sustainability
- Form expectations about the future
- Communicate insights to support decisions

To reflect this reality, the course is organized into eight modules that simulate this professional workflow. The goal is to help you develop not just technical skills, but also:

- Critical thinking
- Professional judgment
- The ability to communicate insights effectively

## Module Structure and Learning Journey

### 🔍 Stage 1: Thinking Like an Analyst (Modules 1–2)

You will begin by learning how to approach financial statements critically.

- Understand that financial reports are shaped by managerial incentives
- Learn how the three core statements are constructed and connected
- Develop a "detective mindset" when reading financial data

> [!tip] What you gain
> A foundation for questioning and interpreting financial information, rather than accepting it at face value.

### 📊 Stage 2: Building Your Analytical Toolkit (Module 3)

Next, you will learn the core quantitative tools used in financial analysis.

- Ratio analysis (profitability, liquidity, efficiency)
- Trend analysis over time
- Common-size comparisons

> [!tip] What you gain
> The ability to systematically break down financial statements and identify patterns.

### 🌐 Stage 3: Understanding Context (Module 4)

Financial numbers only make sense within the right business and industry context.

- Industry structure and competition
- Business models and strategy
- Benchmarking against peers

> [!tip] What you gain
> The ability to interpret financial data meaningfully, not just mechanically.

### 🔍 Stage 4: Interpreting Performance and Risk (Modules 5–6)

You will move beyond calculation to judgment and evaluation.

- Assess the quality and sustainability of earnings
- Identify financial, operational, and strategic risks
- Recognize potential accounting distortions

> [!tip] What you gain
> A deeper understanding of what the numbers really mean — and what they might hide.

### 🔮 Stage 5: Looking Forward (Module 7)

You will use what you have learned to predict the future.

- Build financial forecasts
- Estimate firm value using valuation models

> [!tip] What you gain
> The ability to turn historical analysis into forward-looking decisions.

### 📝 Stage 6: Communicating Like a Professional (Module 8)

Finally, you will learn how to present your analysis clearly and persuasively.

- Structure a professional financial analysis report
- Support conclusions with evidence
- Communicate insights to stakeholders

> [!tip] What you gain
> The ability to produce work similar to what professional analysts deliver.

## What You Will Be Able to Do

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

1. Read and critically evaluate financial statements
2. Apply key analytical tools to assess performance
3. Interpret results within a strategic and industry context
4. Evaluate earnings quality and risk
5. Forecast financial performance and value a company
6. Communicate your findings in a professional report

## Materials

Everything you need is provided in the course notes — no external textbook is required. For those who want optional, deeper background:

- **Recommended (optional, not required):** *Financial Statement Analysis: A Practitioner's Guide* (3rd Ed, Fridson & Alvarez, Ch. 13–14) and *Understanding Financial Statements* (12th Ed, Fraser & Ormiston, Ch. 5)
- **Course running example:** Tesla, Inc. FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC EDGAR, accession `0001628280-26-003952`), benchmarked against peer **BYD** starting in Module 4
- **Case Studies:** Sunbeam, Enron, Oxford Health Plans (referenced in [[M1-Obj-1-2]])

## Why This Matters

Different stakeholders — managers, investors, lenders, and regulators — use financial analysis for different purposes. Throughout the course, you will learn how to:

- Adjust your analysis depending on the audience
- Focus on profitability, risk, or growth as needed
- Support real-world decision-making

> [!quote]
> This course teaches you not just how to analyze numbers, but how to think, decide, and communicate like a professional financial analyst.
