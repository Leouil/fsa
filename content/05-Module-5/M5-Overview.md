# Module 5: Advanced Performance Assessment

> [!abstract] Module Purpose
> Module 4 closed the *context* gap — industry structure, strategy, and peer benchmarks told us whom Tesla competes against and how well it does. This module turns inward with a blunt premise: **reported net income is an accounting estimate, not an economic fact.** Three escalating tests dismantle the illusions one at a time — line-item **accrual** tests (LO 5.1), **cash** verification that kills the EBITDA shortcut (LO 5.2), and **normalization** of accounting discretion, pro forma packaging, and nonrecurring charges into sustainable core earnings (LO 5.3). The deliverable is a reconstructed **True Sustainable Core Earnings** figure — and the confidence to defend it against a press release.
>
> The running example stays **Tesla, Inc.** (FY2025 Form 10-K); **BYD** returns for a single cash-flow contrast in LO 5.2.

> [!success] Key Findings from the Tesla Application
> - **Clean reporter, concentrated judgment**: Tesla shows none of the classic revenue abuses — direct sales, DSO ≈17 days, point-in-time recognition. The live estimates are the **warranty reserve** (up ~146% in two years), **depreciation lives** (5–30-year AI-infrastructure assets), and **regulatory credits** (\$1,993M ≈ **46% of operating income**)
> - **EBITDA is not cash**: Tesla's EBITDA fell **22.5%** across 2023–25 while free cash flow *rose* **42.8%**. EBITDA (\$10,503M) sits *below* CFO (\$14,747M) because it ignores interest income (\$1,680M), stock-based compensation, and working capital. Cash Flow Adequacy eased **1.29× → 1.05×**
> - **The BYD contrast**: a comfortably positive operating margin (and EBITDA) coexists with **free cash flow ≈ −¥97.7B**, as CapEx (≈19.5% of revenue) swamps even a cash-releasing conversion cycle — exactly what an EBITDA-only lender would miss
> - **Packaging is management-controlled**: Tesla's own "Adjusted EBITDA" (the CEO-award milestone, targets rising to \$400B) **excludes stock-based compensation** (≈\$2.8B, ≈65% of operating income); its 2023 net income of \$14,974M was inflated by a **one-time \$5,001M tax benefit**

> [!tip] From Measurement to Skepticism
> Modules 2–4 taught you to *read* the statements and *place* the company. Module 5 teaches you to *distrust* the numbers intelligently: every reported figure is a management estimate until an accrual test, a cash test, and a normalization test say otherwise. A conclusion that survives all three is one you can defend.
