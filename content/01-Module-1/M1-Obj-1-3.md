# Learning Objective 1.3

## Locate and navigate corporate financial disclosures

---

## Instructional Summary

### Where to Find Financial Statements

Publicly held companies in the U.S. must prepare statements according to **Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)**. These statements are filed with the **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** and are publicly accessible.

> [!info] Key Resource
> The SEC's **EDGAR database** (Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval) is the primary source for corporate filings: https://www.sec.gov/edgar/searchedgar/companysearch

### Filing Types

| Filing | Frequency | Contents |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **10-K** | Annual | Comprehensive financial data, business description, risk factors |
| **10-Q** | Quarterly | Unaudited financial statements, MD&A |
| **8-K** | As needed | Material events (acquisitions, leadership changes, etc.) |

### Anatomy of a 10-K

A 10-K is a standardized document containing 15 mandated items. For instance, please navigate a recent 10-K filing for the EV car manufacturer **Tesla, Inc**.
[Inline Viewer: Tesla, Inc. 10-K 2025-12-31](https://www.sec.gov/ix?doc=/Archives/edgar/data/0001318605/000162828026003952/tsla-20251231.htm)

The most important sections for analysis are:

#### Part I

| Item | Contents | Why It Matters |
|------|----------|----------------|
| **Item 1: Business** | Company description, products, markets | Understand the business model |
| **Item 1A: Risk Factors** | Key risks disclosed by management | Identify threats to value |
| **Item 2: Properties** | Physical locations and assets | Assess capital intensity |

#### Part II

| Item | Contents | Why It Matters |
|------|----------|----------------|
| **Item 5: Market** | Stock price data, trading volume | Valuation context |
| **Item 7: MD&A** | Management's discussion of analysis | ==Management's narrative on performance== |
| **Item 8: Financial Statements** | Balance Sheet, Income Statement, Cash Flow, Equity | **Primary data for analysis** |

> [!tip] Start Here
> When reading a 10-K, begin with **Item 7 (MD&A)** and **Item 8 (Financial Statements)** — these are the analytical core.

#### Part III

| Item | Contents | Why It Matters |
|------|----------|----------------|
| **Item 10: Directors & Officers** | Governance information | Assess leadership |
| **Item 11: Executive Compensation** | Pay structures | Align incentives with performance |

#### Part IV

| Item | Contents | Why It Matters |
|------|----------|----------------|
| **Exhibits & Schedules** | Contracts, subsidiaries, warranties | Detailed disclosures |

### The Four Core Financial Statements

1. **Balance Sheet** — Financial position at a point in time (Assets = Liabilities + Equity)
2. **Income Statement** — Performance over a period (Revenue − Expenses = Net Income)
3. **Statement of Stockholders' Equity** — Changes in equity accounts
4. **Statement of Cash Flows** — Cash inflows/outflows from operating, investing, financing

### Supplementary Information

- **Notes to Financial Statements**: Accounting policies, inventory, debt, contingencies — ==an integral part of the report==
- **Auditor's Report**: Independent opinion on fairness
  - Unqualified (clean) — best outcome
  - Qualified — some issues noted
  - Adverse — significant problems
  - Disclaimer — unable to form opinion
- **Management Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)**: Management's perspective on trends and uncertainties related to liquidity, capital resources, and results of operations
- **Proxy Statement**: Director compensation, executive pay, audit-related fees

### Accounting Concepts and Assumptions

- **Accrual Basis**: Revenues recognized when earned, expenses when incurred, regardless of cash flow
- **Matching Principle**: Expenses matched with the revenues they helped generate
- **Materiality**: Only information significant enough to influence a decision must be reported
- **Going Concern Assumption**: The belief that the firm will operate indefinitely

## Practice Activity

> [!example] Practice: Obtain and Analyze a 10-K
>
> 1. Navigate to SEC EDGAR: https://www.sec.gov/edgar/searchedgar/companysearch
> 2. Search for a company by name or ticker symbol (e.g., **Tesla, Inc**, with ticker: **TSLA**)
> 3. Find the most recent 10-K filing
> 4. Locate and identify the four core financial statements
> 5. Locate and identify the notes to financial statements
>
> 6. Check the auditor's report — is the opinion unqualified? (Note the auditor's name and the date of the auditor's opinion)

## Review Questions

> [!question]- What is the difference between a 10-K and a 10-Q?
> A 10-K is filed annually with audited financial statements; a 10-Q is filed quarterly with unaudited statements.

> [!question]- Why is the MD&A section important?
> It provides management's narrative on performance, trends, and uncertainties — the context behind the numbers.

> [!question]- What does an unqualified auditor's opinion mean?
> It means the auditor believes the financial statements are fairly presented in accordance with GAAP.

> [!question]- Why are the notes to financial statements important?
> They contain accounting policies, contingencies, and details that significantly affect interpretation of the face statements.
